# Exact State Reduction for Adaptive Prefix-Cache Replay

**Abstract.** A prefix-cache scheduler can choose among request orders with equal immediate prefill cost by replaying recent demand from the cache each order would leave. Much of that replay can be redundant. For a specified finite continuation, we show that the identities and absolute recency ranks of its resident prefixes suffice to evaluate an observation-based adaptive selector exactly under atomic prefix admission and ordinary leaf LRU. A sufficient survival condition gives the miss count directly. The resulting algorithm shares scores across candidate exits while committing the original policy’s chosen full state. On previously inspected Mooncake traffic, the optimized evaluator reproduced the reference in 8,766 cohort–capacity comparisons and reduced mean native scheduler CPU at 128 blocks from 283.03 to 68.72 µs per cohort. The scheduling result had a narrower scope. History-informed tie-breaking saved prefill positions on Mooncake tool/agent traffic, tied on conversation traffic, and saved zero over exact immediate ordering on either external Qwen/Bailian episode. The evidence supports exact evaluation of a specified continuation and a measured reduction in its computational cost. The value of that continuation as a predictor remains workload-dependent. ## The repeated evaluation problem

Serving two requests with different prefixes can leave the same set of blocks in opposite recency order. The current group may cost the same either way. A later request can then evict a different block. The scheduler considered here evaluates such choices by simulating a finite, already observed workload from each possible exit. Inside that simulation the next request itself depends on which prefixes are cached. Consequently replaying the request order observed from one exit against a second exit can give the wrong score.

Let $q$ denote a ready cohort and $a$ a legal permutation. Starting from the current cache, let $w_a$ be the immediate missing-block count and $F_a$ the complete exit. Write $\mathcal A_0=\arg\min_a w_a$. In the frozen application there are at most four ready requests. The scheduler forms $m\leq 8$ disjoint pairs of completed cohorts from the most recent sixteen, preserving chronological order within each pair. Each pair starts independently at $F_a$. For $m>0$ the secondary objective is

$$
a^*\in\underset{a\in\mathcal A_0}{\arg\min}\;
    \sum_{j=1}^{m} J(F_a,\mathcal H_j).
$$

Averaging would give the same choice. Ties favor the deterministic exact-immediate incumbent and then the earliest arrival-lexicographic order. With no pair, the incumbent wins. The problem is to return that exact order while evaluating fewer hypothetical continuations. Only completed historical requests are available to the continuation estimator at dispatch. [Frozen scheduling contract](../exit_cache_20261108/contract.md) · [Evaluator specification](../exact_tie_20261108/protocol.md)

For example, three distinct one-block requests served from cold at capacity three can leave $(a,x,y)$ or $(a,y,x)$ at the same immediate cost. Consider a historical pair whose first cohort contains $b,a,c,d$ in arrival order and whose second contains only $a$. Its support has four labels, so the direct-score certificate cannot apply. Both exits have the same projected key, with $a$ at rank one. Under the archived k-LPM rule the pair costs three misses from either exit. One replay supplies that score for both. This is a consequence of the transition rule, independent of the empirical results.

## Exact evaluation of a finite continuation

Let a node identify an entire prefix, so two equal final blocks reached through different parents are different nodes. A reachable cache of capacity $C\ge1$ is a prefix-closed tuple $F$ of at most $C$ distinct nodes in retention order, most recent first. A request exposes its capped path $P=(p_1,\dots,p_l)$ from shallow to deep, with $l\le C$. Hits are determined at entry. All nodes of $P$ receive a single fresh service stamp and are protected during admission. Ancestors precede descendants among nodes with the same stamp. The resulting transition and missing-block cost are

$$
T_P(F)=\operatorname{take}_C\big(P\mathbin\Vert (F\setminus P)\big),
\qquad c_P(F)=l-|P\cap F|. \tag{1}
$$

Here deletion from a tuple preserves the order of surviving entries. An ancestor’s last-touch stamp is at least its descendant’s. Equal-stamp nodes lie on one request path. Hence the last node in retention order is a leaf, and while the tuple exceeds capacity it is outside the protected path. Truncation implements the specified oldest-leaf eviction with its depth tie rule. For a request of length $L$ and block width $b$, prefill work is $L-bl+b\,c_P(F)$. The first term includes any compulsory final token, incomplete block and tokens beyond the cap. It does not affect comparisons between serving orders that process the same requests. [Original cache argument](../exit_cache_20261108/review.md) · [Implemented transition](../exit_cache_20261108/engine.cpp)

Fix a finite continuation workload $\mathcal H$. Let $U$ contain every node any permitted continuation access can touch. Write $r_F(x)$ for the one-based position of $x$ in $F$, or $\infty$ if absent. The key $\Pi_U(F)$ stores each resident member of $U$ with its **absolute** rank. Given $U$, every unlisted label is absent. Rank gaps retain the number of intervening outsiders while discarding their identities. All comparisons below fix the capacity, namespace, paths and continuation rules.

**Theorem 1 (projected continuation).** Suppose the selector’s next action and internal update depend only on the pending workload, fixed metadata, its internal state, and cache observations computable from $\Pi_U$, including the chosen action’s work. Couple stochastic selectors with the same random stream. From equally initialized selector states and $\Pi_U(F_1)=\Pi_U(F_2)$, the two executions take the same actions, see the same permitted observations and incur identical total work $J(F_1,\mathcal H)=J(F_2,\mathcal H)$, pathwise under the coupling.

*Proof.* First fix a possible next path $P$. The key determines which of its labels are resident. Prefix closure makes these labels an initial segment, which gives both its hit length and its work by (1). After the atomic update the rank of $p_i$ is $i$. For an old resident $x\in U\setminus P$ with rank $r$, its position before truncation is

$$
\tilde r=l+r-\big|\{p\in P:r_F(p)\le r\}\big|. \tag{2}
$$

Indeed, old copies of earlier path nodes disappear from ahead of $x$, and the whole path is prepended. Its new rank is $\tilde r$ if $\tilde r\le C$, and $\infty$ otherwise. An absent tracked label outside $P$ stays absent. Thus work and projected successor for **every candidate action** are determined by the initial key. From equal keys the selector receives equal observations. Its shared pending set, internal state and random stream therefore give the same next request, internal updates and pending-set evolution. Equation (2) gives equal successor keys, so induction over the finite dispatches yields the claim. Any stopping rule used in this extension must itself depend only on the same permitted information. $\square$

The archived k-LPM continuation alternates oldest pending request with longest retained prefix, resolving ties by arrival order and resetting the alternation each cohort. The theorem also covers selectors that change their priority after an observed miss, or randomize among tied hit lengths using an equally initialized generator. The extension uses the familiar principle of closing an observation map under each transition and then composing it with an observation-based controller. The model-specific step is (2) for protected, atomic prefix paths. The support $U$ is known because these are already recorded historical requests. Using the theorem requires no prediction of the identity of a later live request. ## Direct scoring and action recovery

**Proposition 2 (direct score).** For this result take $U$ to be precisely the union of the paths that will all be served. Let $u=|U|$, $h=|F\cap U|$ and, for $h>0$, $z=\max_{x\in F\cap U}r_F(x)-h$ and set $z=0$ if $h=0$. If $u+z\le C$, the total missing-block count is exactly $u-h$ in every permitted order.

*Proof.* An initially resident tracked node awaiting its first touch has at most $z$ outsiders ahead of it at entry. Later requests can move tracked nodes ahead of it, but cannot move a new outsider ahead. Before any truncation, at most $u-1$ other tracked nodes can precede it, so its rank stays at most $u+z\le C$. Once touched, a tracked node has only tracked nodes ahead of it, including after each subsequent path prepending. Its rank stays at most $u$. Thus each initially absent label misses once on its first appearance and each other occurrence hits. $\square$

For an empty continuation $U$ is empty and the charge is zero. The argument also admits empty paths, partial cache occupancy and repeated requests. Use the capped paths to form $U$, and charge any compulsory remainder separately. With a strict superset of the labels actually touched, $u-h$ could count unvisited labels. Failing the test calls for exact scoring. For example, with $C=2$, $F=(x,a)$ and singleton requests $a,b$, the test fails because $u+z=3$. Serving $a$ first still costs exactly $u-h=1$ miss.

**From scores to the committed action.** Retain the deterministic immediate incumbent. Enumerate all immediately optimal orders, keeping the incumbent when eligible for a complete exit, otherwise its arrival-lexicographically earliest representative. For each historical pair start afresh from each candidate exit. Score the distinct keys for that pair, using Proposition 2 when certified, then assign the resulting scalar to each candidate with that key. A sum term common to all candidates may be skipped in decision-only mode and must be restored for absolute comparisons. Different pairs can induce different classes. Theorem 1 preserves each candidate’s sum, so the original incumbent/lexicographic tie priorities select the identical order. With no complete pair the incumbent wins. Commit that order’s **own complete exit**. Induction with the same realized incoming cohorts then preserves the reference policy’s full trajectory. This property does not imply that its locally immediate-optimal choice will minimize realized episode work. [Exact implementation and policy tie rule](../exact_tie_20261108/evaluator.cpp)

**Where the reduction stops.** The following are mathematical examples using independent single-block roots and capacity two.

- From $(a,b)$ and $(b,a)$, serving $c,a$ costs one and two misses. The starts have the same residents, and $a$ is the sole resident relevant label in both. Its absolute rank makes the difference. For the shorter horizon consisting only of $a$, those unequal keys yield the same zero cost. The key is sufficient and can distinguish more states than a fixed horizon needs.
- From $(a,x)$ and $(a,y)$ the keys for $U=\{a,b,c\}$ coincide. A selector that inspects outsider $x$ and serves $b,c,a$ when it is present, otherwise $a,b,c$, incurs three and two misses. Equal cache keys alone cannot enforce equal actions for this selector.
- Serving only $a$ from $(x)$ and $(y)$ gives the same score and terminal key, with complete exits $(a,x)$ and $(a,y)$. A later real request for $x$ distinguishes them. The stored scalar for a historical pair is reusable across its key class. The physical exit carried forward is specific to the chosen action.

## Relation to existing exact abstractions

Mattson, Gecsei, Slutz and Traiger use LRU stack position to evaluate a recorded address sequence across storage capacities. Touzeau, Maïza, Monniaux and Reineke describe a closer abstraction. To classify a scalar access to one address, their focused semantics retain its set of younger addresses, or an absence marker. Thus two full stacks can already represent the same exact cache observation. Section 4.2 attributes this construction to the authors’ earlier work. [Mattson et al., *Evaluation techniques for storage hierarchies*, Fig. 7](https://doi.org/10.1147/sj.92.0078) · [Touzeau et al., *Fast and exact analysis for LRU caches*, §§4.1–4.3, manuscript pp. 11–13](https://valentintouzeau.fr/papers/POPL2019.pdf#page=11)

**Table 1. The representation and its closest inspected precedents.**

| Aspect | Closest existing result | Present method | Substantive difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cache transition | One scalar reference moves a block to the front of the LRU stack. | Charge misses against the **entry** state for the whole path, then admit its ordered prefix atomically. | Serial touches can reproduce the final order yet charge different work. |
| Continuation | Mattson evaluates a fixed trace. Touzeau propagates possible accesses along a supplied control-flow graph. | The continuation can choose the next pending request using cache observations. | The proof must preserve both the observed hit prefixes and the subsequent choices. |
| Retained observations | Touzeau keeps younger block identities for one focus address. | Keep the joint absolute ranks of resident identities in a known access set. | Outsiders never receive touches in that continuation. Their counts ahead of tracked nodes suffice. |
| Candidate initial states | Stack processing shares evaluation across capacities. Focused analysis collects possible states at a program point. | Several reachable exits at one capacity compete for one dispatch decision. | Group exits separately for each continuation and commit the selected order’s own complete exit when committing the real decision. |
| Direct scoring | In the focused update, the number of distinct younger blocks determines survival of the focus. | A sufficient joint survival test yields the whole continuation’s miss count as the number of initially absent accessed identities. | One certificate can dispense with replay for every request in that continuation. |
| Guarantees and cost | The cited algorithms compute stack statistics or classify possible hits and misses exactly for their models. | The evaluator preserves a finite cost and its deterministic tie priority. | Replay counts depend on distinguishable projected classes and certificates. End-to-end acceleration also depends on classification overhead. |

For a tracked resident $x$, its rank specifies the cardinality of its younger set, and the ranks of other tracked residents identify exactly which of them are younger. The difference counts younger outsiders. This uses the fixed access set to erase outsider identities from the usual stack analysis. Once the per-action cost and successor projection are well defined, extension to a controller that observes only preserved information follows by ordinary composition. Givan, Dean and Greig give the more general stability criterion for stochastic transitions between equivalence classes and class-constant rewards. The cache-specific work here is to exhibit a stable representation for atomic path admission, prove the joint survival certificate, and exploit both across candidate exits. This is a useful specialization of exact abstraction. The inspected passages establish no priority for a new general principle or globally smallest representation. [Givan et al., *Equivalence notions and model minimization in Markov decision processes*, p. 178](https://ics.uci.edu/~dechter/courses/ics-295/fall-2019/papers/givan-dean-greig.pdf#page=16) · [Earlier source inspection record](../exact_tie_20261108/derivation.md)

For a concrete distinction between transition models, start at $(b,a)$ with capacity two and request path $(a,x)$, with $x$ a child of $a$. Atomic charging gives one miss. Touching $x$ then $a$ as scalar references produces the same final stack but evicts $a$ before its touch and charges two misses. The earlier source audit inspected Mattson et al. at Fig. 7. The present overlap check revisited the focused semantics and the stability criterion. Priority for the joint certificate in this exact prefix model remains unresolved. ## Algorithm and computational cost

For each immediately optimal full exit, the reference executes each historical pair in full. The optimized evaluator first identifies ranks that differ across those exits. For each pair it checks whether any differing rank contains a label in that pair’s support $U$. If none does, every exit has the same projected ranks and the same pair score. Otherwise it applies the survival test at each exit. For uncertified exits it forms a key of the varying relevant (rank, identity) entries and stores the score from the first full representative under that key. Fixed ranks are common to all candidates, so omitting them from this per-pair lookup preserves equality of the full projections. The memo is discarded before the next pair. [Native candidate enumeration, closed form and memo](../exact_tie_20261108/evaluator.cpp)

**Common work.** Let $k$ be the current cohort size, $V$ the number of cached partial-order nodes constructed, $A$ the number of immediately optimal complete orders, and $E\le A$ the number of distinct complete exits among them. Both matched evaluators pay for the same incumbent search, traversal and representative selection. In the worst case $V\le\sum_{d=0}^{k} k!/(k-d)!$ and $A\le k!$. Each new transition scans at most $C$ retained blocks plus a path of length at most $C$. The implementation also computes path overlaps, template priorities and lower bounds to find the incumbent. Its linear comparisons for full-state deduplication can cost $O(AEC)$ in addition to the shared search. Cached partial states occupy $O(VC)$ slots when the compiled state extent tracks capacity. Immediate optimality can prune search for a better incumbent without ruling out tied exits needed in the second stage. [Search and deduplication](../exact_tie_20261108/evaluator.cpp)

**Historical work.** For one pair let $B$ count occurrences of capped-path labels, $g$ be its number of requests and $R$ an upper bound on the cost of one full replay. With at most $k_h$ requests in a historical cohort, a tight implementation of the frozen k-LPM rule can mark the cache, compare pending prefix-hit lengths by binary search, and apply an admission in $O(C+k_h\log(C+1))$ per dispatch. Thus $R=O(g[C+k_h\log(C+1)])$ when the physical state extent scales with $C$. Both evaluators use the same continuation. A straightforward materialization of $U$ from dense labels takes $O(B)$ visits with reusable membership scratch. In the actual optimized implementation each arriving cohort updates membership bits and adjacent-pair union sizes, and expiry clears its outgoing bits. This charges time proportional to the incoming and outgoing path occurrences, amortized dense-index growth, and history storage. It avoids rebuilding a set for every candidate. [Continuation implementation](../exit_cache_20261108/engine.cpp) · [History ring and rank differences](../exact_tie_20261108/evaluator.cpp)

On scored pairs the reference performs $E$ full replays. The optimized evaluator spends $O(EC)$ once per decision identifying varying ranks. On a nonconstant pair, let $L$ be the number of exits that fail the direct test and $D\le L$ their number of distinct projected keys. It spends at most $O(EC)$ on rank scans for direct tests and key construction. It executes $D$ replays. Its vector memo uses linear lookup with full key equality, adding up to $O(LDC)$ comparison work per pair. A conservative per-pair bound after the shared pass is therefore
$$
T_{\rm ref}=O(ER),\qquad
T_{\rm opt}=O(EC+LDC+DR).
$$
The reference expression covers the historical stage only. In decision-only mode a common additive term can take zero replays. Obtaining the absolute score can require one representative. If every exit is uncertified and distinguishable, $D=E$ and the optimized evaluator pays for both the full replay count and classification. The analysis gives a reduction in *replay multiplicity* when classes merge, with no uniform total-runtime speedup.

**Storage.** Pair-local keys require up to $O(DC)$ entries in addition to the shared candidate tree, the score vector of length $E$, and $O(C)$ differing ranks and temporary key space. The frozen ring stores the capped paths from its sixteen cohorts. Its membership vector is indexed by every distinct prefix encountered so far in the episode, including expired identities. If $I$ identities have been seen, this contributes $O(I)$ extra entries to the optimized evaluator. The underlying engine also uses persistent interning and transition scratch. The compiled state arrays have a fixed physical extent $C_{\max}$. Copying or allocating a full array may cost $O(C_{\max})$ even with fewer occupied slots. For the two prescribed build families $C_{\max}/C\le4$. Byte capacities, measured RSS and the mathematical size of a projection are consequently different quantities. ## Interpreting the measurements

**What became cheaper.** Per pass at primary capacity, the matched reference and optimized implementations performed the same 226,182 current-order transitions. Historical continuations fell from 420,910 to 46,766. The optimized evaluator used 140,053 direct scores and 55,095 memo hits. Their individual contributions to wall time were not isolated. At primary capacity, the optimized history occupied up to 29,080 bytes versus 9,640 bytes for the reference, and peak native RSS was 3.97 versus 3.66 MiB. The largest acceleration occurred on conversation traffic, where history saved zero prefill. On tool/agent traffic the native reference, optimized evaluator and exact immediate baseline averaged 284.26, 89.21 and 9.44 µs CPU per cohort, respectively. Thus optimized evaluation was 3.19× faster than the matched reference there and still cost 9.45× the CPU of exact immediate scheduling. At 32 blocks the pooled CPU speedup was 1.88×. The Mooncake caller-wall measurement includes IPC and response decoding. External in-process wall measures from ready cohort input through committed state. Comparisons of speed use matched boundaries within a study. [Native counts, timings, and tails](../exact_tie_20261108/benchmark_summary.json) · [Timing boundaries](../external_tie_20261109/protocol.md)

**Controls and transfer.** On external chat, exact immediate ordering, k-LPM and the rank-weighted history control all matched tie-only on primary prefill. Three registered history-blind draws among distinct optimal exits respectively tied it or saved a further 3,072 and 3,584 positions. These draws show that an attainable exit choice affected this episode. They do not estimate an expected advantage reliably. Against exact immediate ordering, $\Delta P$ was zero for both workloads at both resolutions (512 and 16 tokens) and both retained capacities (65,536 and 16,384 positions). An observed 12,800 reused positions on primary chat describes the tested trajectory and supplies no upper bound on achievable reuse. The API work tie follows from the frozen namespace and cannot resolve tenant-wide sharing. [All episode totals and contrasts](../external_tie_20261109/summary.json) · [Independent audit](../external_tie_20261109/result_audit.md)

**Measurement scope.** Mooncake starts each bin with a cold state, warms it over the first quarter and shares a prefix trie across requests in that bin. Qwen/Bailian preserves state continuously across measured windows and reconstructs cumulative identities within documented chat lineages. Traffic source, namespace, warm-up and episode boundaries therefore change together. The first study's interval concerns its sampled bins in two fixed traces and is sensitive to its single discordant bin. The subsequent Mooncake census and the two external episodes give finite-workload contrasts. Timing repetitions add no independent traffic samples. [Mooncake design and prefix interning](../commitment_20261108/study.py) · [Census design](../exit_cache_20261108/contract.md) · [External panel and provenance](../external_tie_20261109/source/provenance.md)

Mean intentional gate waits were 272.4 ms per external chat request and 68.2 ms per API request, separately from scheduler computation. At primary capacity the optimized process peaked at 5.66 and 4.53 MiB RSS, respectively. At native resolution and the same retained-token capacity the peaks rose to 120.94 and 97.52 MiB. Against exact immediate ordering the external $\Delta P$ was zero and incremental decision wall was positive, so no marginal prefill time offsets the overhead in a serialized break-even calculation. Only policies using the identical gate share its collection delay. These studies supply no measurements of accelerator latency, production throughput, energy or monetary value. ## Limitations and conclusion

Exactness applies to the declared protected admission, tie ordering within a service stamp, capacity and observable selector. A selector that uses unprojected state, an admission policy that evicts by frequency, or a cost that depends on device timing would require its own preservation argument. The projection may distinguish exits whose cost coincides for a particular horizon. Conversely, the small-state checks and complete recorded trajectories test the implementation but cannot establish a universal result. In particular, choosing the access set from only one realized adaptive trajectory would leave other permitted branches uncovered. The proof requires a set that covers every possible access in the comparison. [Proof fixtures and archived audit](../exact_tie_20261108/audit.md)

Storage becomes especially visible at fine resolution. In continuous native-resolution chat, the optimized index ended with 1,620,275 slots including its sentinel, while only 5,696 identities were active in the retained history. These counts illustrate why pair-local memoization and a sixteen-cohort window can coexist with episode-long index growth. The empirical comparison measured a bundle of exact shortcuts. Its aggregate timings do not apportion savings among direct scoring, memo hits and skipped common terms. [External memory and mechanism table](../external_tie_20261109/final_report.md) · [Native evaluator counters](../exact_tie_20261108/benchmark_summary.json)

A cache state can be large even when only a small part of it can affect a specified continuation. Absolute ranks preserve the influence of unobserved entries through the space they occupy. This observation permits exact multi-exit scoring under atomic path admission, with a work bound in terms of the number of uncertified, distinct projections. The implementation demonstrates a computational gain on an existing evaluator. Whether that evaluator's choice improves the next real episode is a separate empirical question, and the external episodes delimit the evidence for investing in the historical tie-breaker itself.
