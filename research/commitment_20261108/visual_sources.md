## One question and contribution map

For documented, executable biomedical metadata cohorts, when a 2D brush and shortened description are compatible with several source-table filters, can an LLM assistant use three truthful case-membership clarifications to reduce error in the reported finite cohort count relative to exact Bayesian active surveying and a generic same-tool assistant? Its mechanism is to minimize the largest count range still compatible with the answers and a declared bound on row-level exceptions. Lasso cardinality can overcount neighboring points and omit distant members. The independently executable hidden rule plus a precommitted exception mask determines truth. No assertion concerns biological validity, statistical generalization to a new population, or actual human lasso intent.

**Known** – brush autocomplete, chart clarifications, and optimal Bayesian reduction in count uncertainty all exist. Oracle access to the complete intended rule makes the count trivial. **Limitation** – a posterior over a possibly misspecified catalog can put very little mass on an admissible filter whose count differs greatly. A numerical checker does not identify which hidden cohort the user intended. **Proposal** – compute an exact partially identified set and choose query cases to contract its worst-case width, using row-equivalence compression. **Prediction** – on held-out predicate families with structurally ambiguous text/brush and budgeted exceptions, small-budget count error may be lower when language priors are wrong and competing counts are far apart. **Defeaters** – calibrated priors can favor Bayesian surveying, all admissible counts may coincide, the bounded exception model may fail, or direct clarification of the complete filter may be feasible. There is no new claim for expected information gain generally.

**Derivation.** Let H be a fixed finite catalog of unique binary masks on N source rows. Unknown binary membership y is within Hamming distance s of at least one h. In a truthful transcript let T be the observed positive count. For h let e be its mismatches on distinct queried rows and p,n its positive/negative counts on unqueried rows. Discard h if e>s. Otherwise its possible total counts are exactly every integer from T+p−min(p,s−e) through T+p+min(n,s−e). For either extremum flip up to s−e unqueried bits of one sign. Intermediate integers use fewer flips. The union over h is sharp and may have gaps. The real-valued minimax absolute-error estimate is the midpoint of its extreme endpoints; apply deterministic nearest-integer rounding for count reporting, with sharp integer radius ceil((U−L)/2) for hull endpoints L,U. The diameter of the hull is a deterministic conditional quantity, not a confidence interval. If the union is empty, report the assumption conflict and the trivial observed-label range [T,T+N−number queried]. A compatible transcript alone does not establish the assumption. When no plausible s is available, use this solely as sensitivity analysis.

At leaves the value is that hull diameter. At earlier nodes take the minimum over unqueried row signatures of the maximum continuation value over the feasible response branches. A row signature is its bit vector across all candidate masks. Permuting rows of the same signature preserves all Hamming constraints and counts, so query one representative per signature while retaining its unqueried multiplicity. Fixed-depth-three evaluation is practicable for small catalogs and a moderate number of distinct signatures. For two centers more than 2s apart, ≤2s membership queries admit a path consistent with both centers. On disagreements the adversary alternates which center to contradict, and on agreements matches both. Conversely, 2s+1 distinct disagreements suffice to identify which center's ball contains y. This guarantees center identification in the two-center case only. Residual count ambiguity persists within a ball. For s=0 an explicit intended filter settles the query; for unconstrained s, unseen memberships can be freely varied. No novel general minimax decision-tree theorem is claimed.
