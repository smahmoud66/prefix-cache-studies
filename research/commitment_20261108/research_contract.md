# Research contract · November 8, 2026 · before evaluation

**Selected question.** For a batch-tolerant inference service processing anonymized Mooncake conversation and tool/agent arrivals, does exact, certificate-pruned ordering of each small ready cohort reduce total prompt positions requiring prefill relative to the best visible-cohort heuristic order, with identical cache capacity, eviction and arrival information? We expect a gain when a long prompt's protected path would evict a shorter ready prefix. The study distinguishes an immediate ordering gain from shifting a cache miss to the next cohort. A small established ceiling on further savings would explain when an inexpensive scheduler suffices.

**Scientific investment.** Each released request exposes actual arrival time, prompt length and reusable prefix hashes. These identify work in a specified prefill model without generating an answer. The question can yield both an achievable result and a ceiling on any legal improvement in that model. SGLang's DFS, Dexter et al.'s k-LPM, and PEEK already exploit locality. Fixed-order caching and bounded-delay page scheduling also have offline optima. Our proposed contribution is a characterization of how much prompt work *remains recoverable* after a strong online order selector, using bounds that continue to hold across all within-cohort permutations. Study of this combination is a scoped hypothesis of distinction. The individual search and interval-packing algorithms are classical.

**Mechanism and constructive revision.** From the same entry state simulate five orders, including queue-aware cLPM and a shadow-trie DFS. Their cheapest is the incumbent. Any remaining prefix node absent from the current retained trie must be computed at least once. This bounds the cost of every completion of a partial order, permitting exact branch-and-bound on cohorts of up to four. A single global count of unique prefixes ignores compulsory evictions. We added a second, hindsight-only certificate. Sum the distinct demands in each ordered cohort to obtain D. For consecutive cohort demands of one block add an interval covering their separating cuts. Add an interval from the warm cache to first demand where applicable. Without creating that block again, it must remain resident across the entire interval. At each cut only C such blocks can survive. If H is the maximum cardinality of a subset fitting every cut, at least D−H block computations are required. For a formal charging argument choose the first service demanding each block in each cohort. Each gap between representatives either contains a later creation (including at its right representative), or its interval fits the cut packing. Disjoint gaps per block ensure distinct charges. Initially absent first demands need their own creation. The relaxation grants free within-cohort sharing and discards ancestor restrictions. Even with C=1 and cohorts [A],[B,B],[A] it can be loose. It receives future demand only for evaluation.

A three-block warm cache holding a and the two-block path b/y illustrates the ordering effect. Serving b/y/z then a costs two blocks; a then b/y/z costs one. Conversely, with C=2 and warm a/z, serving a/x before b/t minimizes immediate work but ends with b/t. If the next cohort requires a/x, two extra blocks are needed. **Fatal to a global dominance theorem:** a common-entry per-cohort guarantee does not extend to different exit states. **Bounded correction:** use carried whole-episode work as the primary endpoint and the offline ceiling as a secondary diagnostic. **Empirical uncertainty:** frequency of useful reorderings, strength of the certificate and planner overhead.

**Visualization proposal.** For a misleading 2D brush over a biomedical cohort table, which three row-membership clarifications minimize error in the intended cohort count? DQVis provides real tables and executable chart tasks. Represent possible intentions by a finite catalog of source-table filters, each within s exceptions of the true mask. After j answers, each surviving filter has e mismatches. If its remaining positive and negative counts are P and N, and observed positives are T, the feasible count interval for that filter is [T+P−min(P,s−e), T+P+min(N,s−e)]. Each endpoint is attainable by flipping unqueried rows. Minimize worst-case terminal diameter across a three-question decision tree. Compare absolute count error against exact Bayesian active surveying, a generic LLM given the same tools, masks, descriptions and clarification budget, and an oracle with the true filter. The prediction is lower count error under misspecified intent priors, with possible losses under accurate priors. **Fatal to the original distinction:** Bayes-optimal count clarification is already active surveying. **Bounded correction:** the revision targets worst-case count identification under explicit exceptions. **Uncertainty:** credible s and accuracy under actual analyst feedback. A prospective evaluation could construct hidden executable predicates and exceptions; genuine human intent and perception would remain open. This proposal differs from B in its feedback, target and misspecification model. We selected scheduling because its primary outcome needs fewer constructed behavioral assumptions.

**Historical scope.** The eight-round index records retry/allocation, multi-task training and evaluation, and geometric representation questions. The measured outcome is original prompt work caused by prefix reuse and eviction. The index limits this nonrepetition claim to recorded formulations.

Sample 4 development and 16 evaluation bins per trace from 48; the target is the 44 remaining after development. Include a pressure condition at 32 blocks, plain LRU ablation, k-LPM and cLPM controls, common warm starts and separate transient scratch. All six methods use one configuration. Published PEEK uses finer-grained prefix information, additional lane/guard mechanisms and different scheduling interfaces. Our block-level counterparts test the visible mechanisms and do not reproduce their systems.
